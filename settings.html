Setup

Three steps, in order: connect your watch, pull your history, then create a plan on the Plan page. Runalytics has something to show from the second step onward, and something to train with after the third.

    Training zones

    The boundaries behind the dashboard's training-status chart, recovery bands and weekly load target. Runalytics ships COROS's published numbers — move them only if you know why, because every zone in the app follows these values.

    Training load ratio

    Acute load (7 days) divided by chronic load (42 days) — COROS calls this the Intensity Trend. The four boundaries below cut it into the six published status bands: Decreasing, Resuming/Performance, Maintaining, Optimized, Excessive.

    Above the Optimized limit reads as Excessive, and it is also the point where injury risk starts to climb. COROS shares one range between Resuming and Performance: coming down into it from a higher load reads as Performance, anything else as Resuming.

    Recovery bands

    Your recovery score (sleep, resting heart rate, HRV) split into four bands. Each band starts one point above the limit you set.

    Above Normal reads as Fresh, up to 100.

    Weekly load recommendation

    The 7-day load target on the dashboard, built on a build/recovery cycle: a run of increasing weeks, then one deliberately easier week.

    Quality pacing

    Tempo and interval sessions carry a pace range. A session counts as out of range only when the reps miss it by more than the tolerance below — a near miss is GPS noise, not a wrong plan. If that many quality sessions in a row all miss the same way, the Plan page proposes moving the ranges slightly.

    One session out of range is never a trend — a hill, a group run, a bad night's sleep. Nothing is changed without you accepting it.

    Calendar subscription

    Your plan publishes as an .ics feed your calendar app can subscribe to, so scheduled sessions appear beside everything else you own. The feed refreshes whenever the plan changes or a sync lands.

    Injury assistant

    Optional. Point at any OpenAI-compatible chat endpoint and the Injuries page becomes a chat about your pain, grounded in your training data. Without it, the journal still works and replies come from the built-in logger. The key is stored in the macOS keychain, never in a file.
