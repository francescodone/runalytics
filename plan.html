What is threshold pace?See how it shapes every training pace

Threshold pace is the fastest pace you can hold for about an hour — a comfortably hard effort you could sustain for an hour but not two. It sits right at the lactate threshold, where your body stops clearing lactate as fast as it makes it. In practice it is close to the pace you race a 10K to a half marathon at: someone running 4:45/km threshold is roughly a 48-minute 10K runner and a 1:45 half.

It is the single number the plan is built on. Every pace the sessions prescribe is derived from it, so one honest value here makes the whole plan honest:

  • Easythreshold + 45–75 s
  • Tempothreshold ± 5 s
  • Intervalsabout 25 s faster than threshold
  • Long-run steady stretchesmarathon pace, from the same model

Your watch already measures this: COROS derives it from your best efforts and its fitness assessments, and it fills this field in after a sync. Type over it only if you know the number is stale — a threshold that is too fast makes every easy day hard and every quality day unsustainable, which is the most common way a plan goes wrong.
